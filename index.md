## 📝 Reflection

### 1. Agile Charter

#### Situation:
I was required to create an Agile Charter for my WIL project.

#### Task:
My task was to define the project goals, scope, and approach to developing the system.

#### Action:
I created the Agile Charter based on the needs of the project and the features I wanted to include.

#### Result:
I completed the Agile Charter and gained a better understanding of how project planning helps organise development work and establish goals.

### 2. User Stories and Tasks

#### Situation:
Count4U needed features that would make stock and sales management easier for spaza shop owners.

#### Task:
I needed to identify the users' needs and break the project requirements into manageable tasks.

#### Action:
I created user stories and tasks for features such as managing products, recording sales, updating stock, viewing daily sales, and identifying low-stock products.

#### Result:
I learned how user stories help connect system features to user needs. Breaking the project into smaller tasks also made the development process easier to manage.

### 3. Product Backlog

#### Situation:
I needed to organise the features and requirements for Count4U before and during development.

#### Task:
My task was to create a product backlog and prioritise the features according to their importance.

#### Action:
I added the main project requirements to Trello and used red, orange, and green labels to represent high, medium, and low priorities.

#### Result:
I completed the product backlog and gained a better understanding of how focusing on important features first helps while keeping track of the remaining work.

### 4. Sprint Backlog

#### Situation:
Count4U involved several development and documentation tasks that needed to be completed within the project timeline.

#### Task:
I needed to divide the work into manageable sprints and track the tasks planned for each stage.

#### Action:
I organised the project work into sprint lists on Trello. These included planning, designing the interface, developing the system, testing, preparing documentation, and completing the final submission.

#### Result:
I learned how sprint planning helps break a large project into smaller stages and makes it easier to monitor progress and identify outstanding tasks.

### 5. Agile Logbook

#### Situation:
I needed to keep a record of my progress throughout the development of Count4U.

#### Task:
My task was to document the work completed and the progress made.

#### Action:
I used my Trello board to organise my sprints and track project tasks.

#### Result:
I gained a better understanding of the importance of tracking progress throughout a project. 

### 6. Developing the Count4U System

#### Situation:
Spaza shop owners may have to check their stock manually and use calculators to work out the cost of customer purchases. This can make it more difficult to keep track of stock and daily sales.

#### Task:
I wanted to develop a web-based system that would help spaza shop owners manage their products, stock, and sales more easily.

#### Action:
I developed Count4U using PHP, MySQL, HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and Bootstrap. I worked on features that allow users to manage products and categories, record sales, calculate purchase totals, update stock, and view sales information. I also worked on the interface to make the system easy to use.

#### Result:
I developed a functional web application and deployed it online. This experience helped me understand how the frontend, backend, and database work together to support a real-world need. 

### 7. Wireframes and User Interface Design

#### Situation:
Count4U needed an interface that would be clear and easy for spaza shop owners to use.

#### Task:
I needed to plan the layout and appearance of the system before and during development.

#### Action:
I created low-fidelity and high-fidelity wireframes to plan the layout and user interactions. I used these designs as a guide when developing the website.

#### Result:
I learned how wireframes help organise ideas before implementation and how interface design can make a system easier to understand and navigate.

### 8. Business Case and Business Model

#### Situation:
Count4U was developed to address a problem experienced by small shop owners, so I needed to consider the purpose and value of the solution.

#### Task:
My task was to explain the problem, proposed solution, feasibility, and potential value of the project.

#### Action:
I prepared a business case and business model to explain the purpose of Count4U, its target users, and how the system could provide value to spaza shop owners.

#### Result:
I gained a better understanding of how software projects need to consider both technical requirements and the needs of their intended users.

### 9. Solution Architecture

#### Situation:
Count4U required a structured design showing how its main technical components would work together.

#### Task:
I needed to document the system structure and explain how the application, database, and other components supported its features.

#### Action:
I prepared a solution architecture document to represent the structure of Count4U and the relationship between its main components.

#### Result:
I improved my understanding of how planning a system's structure supports development and helps explain how the different parts of an application work together.

### 10. Project Presentation

#### Situation:
I needed to present Count4U and explain the problem it addresses, its features, and the solution I developed.

#### Task:
My task was to communicate the purpose and functionality of the system clearly.

#### Action:
I prepared a PowerPoint presentation explaining the project, its main features, and its intended users. I also prepared to demonstrate the working system.

#### Result:
I gained experience in explaining a technical project and communicating how it could help solve a real-world problem. I also learned the importance of presenting both the development process and the completed solution.

### 11. Online Deployment

#### Situation:
The Count4U project needed to be accessible online so that it could be viewed and demonstrated without running it only on a local computer.

#### Task:
I needed to deploy the website and provide a working link to the system.

#### Action:
I deployed Count4U online and checked that the website could be accessed through its public link.

#### Result:
I successfully made the system available online. 

### 12. Overall Reflection

Working on Count4U challenged me to manage the planning, design, development, documentation, and deployment of a complete web application. I had to organise my tasks, manage my time, and work through technical challenges involving PHP, MySQL, and the website's functionality.

One of the main lessons I learned was the importance of planning before development and keeping track of progress throughout the project.

I also learned that developing a useful system requires understanding the needs of its intended users, not only focusing on the technical side. Completing and deploying Count4U helped me build my problem-solving skills, improve my understanding of web development, and gain experience in taking a project from an idea to a working solution.

Overall, this project helped me recognise areas where I have improved and areas where I still need to develop my technical skills and confidence. It also gave me practical experience in managing and completing an individual software project.
